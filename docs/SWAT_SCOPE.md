# Research bridge: Turing's 'telepathy-proof room' and SwaT

Primary paper: Abdulrahman Alabdulkareem, Irene Tenison, Yang Zhang, Yada Zhu, Boris Katz, Dan Gutfreund, *Swarm Telepathy: Prefill-Free KV-Cache Transfer Across Families*, arXiv:2610.15095 (submitted 10 October 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15095 .

The authors describe pair-specific learned KV-cache translators for cross-family language-model handoffs, keeping sender and receiver model weights frozen. The paper reports benchmark advantages over text-based handoffs under its tested conditions. It does not demonstrate psychic telepathy, consciousness transfer, human detection, or arbitrary universal cache compatibility.

Our game uses its idea **only as motivation** for a directed optional information edge between machine endpoints. The Lean graph records permissions, not tensors or an operating adapter. A text-relay or shared bytes experiment can establish causal side-channel effects but cannot verify SwaT performance.

Turing's own telepathy passage is historical background; the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's *The Turing Test*, sec. 2.9, addresses his conjectural extrasensory-perception concern and how isolation would alter the experiment: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/turing-test/#ArgExtSenPer .

### Required controls before any KV claim
1. Identify exact model versions, key/value shapes, tokenization and positional alignment, mapper weights and training provenance.
2. Test actual transfer against a text-only and a no-transfer baseline with matched information budget and latency reporting.
3. Include an absent-link machine and a human-with-tool condition separately, where feasible.
4. Inspect hidden-channel event traces, not merely a fluent text answer.
5. Never describe a shared text note or simulated cache payload as transferred KV states.

The paper's favorable aggregate score or speed does not establish the improvement in our Reverse Turing Game. That requires new, preregistered trials.
